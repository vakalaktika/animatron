# Mid-century Ink

Match the bundled references closely. Treat this as a specific illustration grammar, not a broad retro aesthetic. Keep creative freedom in subject, palette, and setting within that grammar. Do not substitute a realistic portrait with vintage texture, a polished vector mascot, or a densely rendered children's-book scene.

## Select the illustration treatment

For new artwork, read `references/style-presets.md` and resolve the treatment without asking: the request's choice, else the project or source treatment, else a saved preference, else **Original / Classic graphic** (`classic-graphic`). **Newer / Detailed storybook** (`detailed-storybook`) is used only when chosen. Revisions and sequels retain their source treatment. Apply the selection to the core brief, prompt scaffold and guide defaults throughout the project, and to every medium: raster, native SVG, rigs and animation. The treatment applies to artwork, not functional UI or document formatting.

Keep the two treatments distinct. Detailed is not Original plus extra detail, and must not inherit Original's angular proportions, dot eyes or wedge noses by accident.

**Original** uses `assets/style-reference.png` plus `assets/reference-8591.jpeg` as its historical pair (see Reference access) and the rules in "The Original look" in `references/style-presets.md`.

**Detailed:**

- For every Detailed generation or edit, read [Detailed: one visual baseline](references/detailed-character-style.md), and view and attach `assets/detailed-primary-scene-v1.jpeg` as the primary STYLE anchor. Ed selected this exact scene to align new artwork. Its face/nose construction, expressive eyes, boundary economy, grouped hair and rendering density govern every visible subject. See `references/detailed-target.json` for binding/provenance. Identity anchors lock only their named subjects.
- For Detailed people, read [Family-anchor face study](references/detailed-face-study.md). For images containing adults, view and attach its two user-selected adult face crops alongside the primary scene; they refine eyes, readable noses and restrained expressions without replacing individual identities. Every visible unobscured human face must have a readable nose; fewer marks must not erase its bridge/tip or profile projection. Do not force equal eye whites, visible wedges or open grins onto the whole cast.
- Apply [Contextual feature choices](references/detailed-feature-choices.md) to select eyes, noses, ears, brows and mouths from identity, age, head angle and the depicted emotion/action, within one proportional drawing system and rendering density. Neutral and task-focused people have visibly open attentive eyes; narrower or closed eyes need an expression/action reason. For story scenes, choose a concise expression cue per visible person from the page's action, carrying accepted identity and proportions across pages. Expression-study characters are demonstrations, not identity or style anchors.
- Retired Detailed assets (`assets/retired/`: mockup 06 and the party/hearth geometry sources) are history only. Never attach them, Original historical images or unapproved trials as positive Detailed inputs, and never average them into the baseline.

## One treatment for the entire cast

The resolved project treatment governs **every visible character**, whether saved, referenced, newly invented, unnamed, recurring, or appearing once: people, pets, animals, monsters, fantasy creatures, talking objects and background crowds, in pictures, vectors, rigs and animated scenes alike. Missing identity references are not a style exception: invent a distinctive identity inside the selected treatment. Do not request a photo or an existing drawing for a wholly invented character.

For Original, construct invented characters with deliberate geometric masses, tapered or angular silhouettes, economical facial marks and flat spot colors; simplify species anatomy within that grammar. For Detailed, use `references/detailed-character-style.md`: natural cartoon proportions, distinct descriptive faces, modest shaped eyes with light areas and dark directed pupils, selective tapered ink and grouped flat shapes. Preserve its locked expressive balance: naturally attentive children, without automatic sleepy lids, under-eye bags or exaggerated wide eyes. Detailed permits rounder forms without requiring every identity to share a round face. Neither mode permits generic glossy mascots, photorealistic anatomy or modeled shading. Preserve approved individual traits; shared style does not mean identical faces or forcing human proportions onto animals.

In **every** generation or edit prompt, name the resolved preset and its construction rules and explicitly apply them to all visible characters, including any invented or background cast. Label references by role: identity references establish who; written treatment rules and available style examples establish how everyone is drawn. A reference character cannot be the only figure following the selected style. Keep the same treatment through character drafts, page art, covers, cards, new poses, rigs, animation and repairs.

Before first scene use, give each invented recurring character a compact project-local design brief (silhouette, face/eyes, color blocks and distinguishing cues) in the selected style. Reuse that provisional brief and available artwork across appearances; do not invent approval or add website records. A one-scene extra needs no separate character sheet or approval cycle. Follow the host's existing approval requirements where applicable.

During the useful-size artwork review, inspect **the entire visible cast** for treatment fidelity, including invented creatures and background extras without anchors. Compare referenced characters to their accepted identity anchors separately. Record actual style observations with the final asset hash; anatomy or identity fidelity alone cannot pass a style mismatch. Repair the drifting character or region and recheck protected details. Never relabel a wrong-style output, switch the project preset, or alter approval metadata to make it pass. Preserve unaffected accepted artwork and the other style's designs. Automatic quality review is never user approval.

## Choose the output workflow

- **Raster illustrations and photo/artwork edits:** follow the Workflow below and the host adapter's image-tool rules.
- **SVGs, editable vectors, site decorations, animation-ready assets:** read `references/svg-and-rigs.md`. Author real vector geometry directly; do not replace requested SVGs with raster images or PNGs embedded in SVG wrappers. An image generator is optional for exploration, not a requirement for native SVG.
- **Design systems, site/app components, presentations, documents:** read `references/design-system-workflow.md`. Build reusable rules and editable outputs appropriate to the medium. Keep the drawing language consistent while preserving readable text, predictable controls, and clear functional states.
- **Characters turning or animated:** begin with the finished key pose and movement plan. Read `references/3d-construction.md` for optional proportion/perspective aids. Use `scripts/turnaround.mjs` when a volume model helps; authored turn drawings are equally valid. Register needed head/eye/mouth/hand drawings at common anchors. Refine projected contours against the illustration before rigging each required view; a flat face rotated in-plane is not a perspective turn.
- **Before rigging ANY subject that moves** (person, animal, creature, object), do a movement study with `references/movement-analysis.md`: classify it, work out how that kind of thing moves, and derive its splits (hinges, ball joints, chains, slides, swap sets such as head views, eyes, mouths and hands, and loose parts with their own timing). Record it in `metadata.motionProfile`. If it is unclear how the thing should move, make a stated creative choice, or ask one short question with concrete options when the choice changes the whole rig.
- **Performances:** build performance rigs with `scripts/perform.mjs`: head split from body with a head swap set registered at the neck (turns lead with the head), eye swaps, and bones for hair, clavicles and garments. Place them with their `metadata.defaultPose`.
- **Animals, creatures and objects:** preserve species anatomy in the illustration. Optional construction: declare the subject as 3D parts for `scripts/figure.mjs` (ellipsoid, frustum, capsule, band, patch, flap, with surface marks; `examples/pip/` is an Original terrier) and use the same turnaround, per-view rigs and checks. Get the species' proportions right in the model first.
- **Four-legged gaits:** `scripts/gait.mjs` (trot, walk; phase-based legs with the right front and hind joint directions, ground and foot lock). Match speeds when characters move together.
- **Walks and other two-legged gaits:** `scripts/walk.mjs` on a profile rig keys contact, down, passing and up, then solves the hips' height so a foot always meets the ground and the travel speed so the planted foot never slides. Walks bake on ones and roll the foot heel to toe; add toe breaks with `scripts/toe.mjs` before walking a figure so the toes stay planted while the heel peels (motion.md, "Feet that roll"). Stop or start with a clip that begins on the walk's pose and pins the front foot (`pinFoot`).
- **Characters that touch or are tied together** (a leash, holding hands, a rider, a prop passed between them): combine them into ONE scene rig with `scripts/scene.mjs` (prefixed ids, offsets, one clock: shorter cycles repeat to the longest, so tune frames per cycle until they divide it, and match travel speeds first). It refuses members drawn in different treatments. Hang ropes, leashes, strings, cables and hoses between two points with `scripts/rope.mjs` (a verlet chain baked to segment bones, seamless on the loop). A hand that holds something while walking carries it (`carryArm` in walk.mjs). `examples/scenes/leash/` is Dot walking Pip on a leash.
- **Movement in general:** read `references/motion.md`: plan key poses in frames, ask for spacing, anticipation, overlap and follow-through, bake them with `scripts/motion.mjs` (Rig 1 interpolates linearly, so never hand-write sparse linear keys for organic motion), turn characters with view swaps, and critique rendered frames by number.
- **Ship animations light:** read `references/size-budget.md`. Plan for the budget from the start (only the views and swap sets the piece uses, rope segments to what reads, cycles that divide the loop), then run `scripts/slim.mjs` LAST on every rig that goes into a composition. Always keep the unslimmed rig as the editable master. Rig 2 currently uses preserve-only slimming to avoid modifying painted registration or drawing states.
- **Animation-ready assets:** include an actual skeleton and artwork bindings in the shared Rig 1 contract: `references/mci-rig-v1.md`. Read the JSON schema and use `scripts/validate-rig.cjs` to validate numeric/hierarchy data when Node is available. The diagnostic fixture is a format example, never a style reference. Keep format `mid-century-ink-rig`: version 1 for existing vector rigs; version 2 for registered drawing states and/or transparent PNG attachments (`references/mci-rig-v2.md`). Plugin release numbers are independent of file-format versions. Never silently downgrade a Rig 2 master. Keep `.mci.json` rigs separate from compositions (such as `.animatron.json`) and rendered media.

Native SVG is a finished deliverable when requested. Apply the same six style checks to its rendered appearance. Never label an unrendered SVG or untested skeleton as visually verified. Follow the current host's file-saving rules. Do not modify a destination app or publish assets unless authorized by the user, and claim import compatibility only after testing it.

## Core style brief: adapt to the selected treatment in every generation prompt

Use graphic 1950s commercial/book illustration with UPA-influenced Cartoon Modernism. Construct every subject according to the selected treatment: deliberate irregular geometric masses and tapered silhouettes for Original; natural cartoon proportions, distinct cheek/jaw turns, decisive tapered contours and grouped directional hair matching the primary scene for Detailed. Retain each approved character's distinct round or angular features. Pair fine open ink strokes with independent flat spot-color fills, paper areas, and selective black anchors. Keep faces economical and unmodeled, each head in one consistent view, graphic depth controlled, complete scenery composed around the action, and negative space intentional. Use dark ink, a clean background, and one or two accent families unless identifying content requires more. Keep texture absent or localized. Avoid realistic anatomy and spherical shading, gradients, glossy vector polish, thick universal outlines, dense surface marks, and blanket grain. Match the selected treatment's active visual references rather than merely signaling a retro mood. For Detailed, the primary scene resolves the exact balance; do not independently maximize geometry or strip every subtle tone if that changes its drawing language.

Treat this brief as the shared baseline, not a substitute for the actual reference images; adapt proportions, silhouette, contours and interior detail to `references/style-presets.md` and the selected visual example. Original uses broad hair/beard masses and minimal interior marks; Detailed permits observed grouped facets and more descriptive contours, keeping flat stylization and approved identity. Apply the following defaults without reproducing every bullet verbatim in the prompt.

## Shared defaults within the selected treatment

- Construct new subjects in the selected treatment; carry accepted character designs forward without redesigning their identity. Original may use tapered wedges, off-center ovals, sharp elbows and long or compressed proportions; do not impose those on Detailed, whose written construction takes precedence. Preserve diverse body types through abstraction rather than forcing everyone into one template.
- Think in 3D, draw in 2D: use volumes to check perspective and consistency. Finish with authored contours that preserve the selected treatment, gesture and identity. Primitive projection is a construction stage, not a required finish.
- Commit every head to ONE view: front, three-quarter, or profile. Eyes, nose, mouth, contour and hair must all agree on which way the face looks. Never combine a front-view face with a profile nose, place the only visible eye on the outline, or run a contour line across the cheek. See Face construction below.
- Use a few expressive facial marks, flat unmodeled skin, and hair/beard masses or groups. Close-ups and detailed photos must not increase realism. Preserve the observed approved face/nose/eye construction, age, complexion, hair, glasses, beard contour and build through economical marks. A new pose or smaller scale does not authorize a generic substitute face.
- Separate fine ink detail from broad fills. Permit open contours, partial line-only objects, paper-colored faces, and selective solid black anchors. Never outline everything with one heavy stroke.
- Keep space shallow and graphic. Compose scenery and props to support the action; simplify incidental marks rather than stripping the setting. Preserve the source's subject, action, relationship, and recognizable setting, not every photographic background detail.
- For a new unrelated project choose a deliberate limited palette fitting the subject; retain accepted palettes in revisions and series. Default to structural black/dark ink, a paper/background field, and one or two chromatic accents. Translate local colors into those roles rather than reproducing every photo color. Allow a richer flat palette only for essential content or a closely matched multicolor reference.
- Apply texture selectively and last. Clean paper and flat fills are valid. Use sparse hatch, stipple, halftone, or uneven ink only where justified. Do not cover the image in grunge or use texture to fake style fidelity.

## Face construction

Applies to every output and both treatments: raster prompts, native SVG, rigs, design-system art. Choose the view from the subject's pose, then place every mark for that view. For vectors and rigs, use the head construction to register features consistently across authored views (`references/3d-construction.md`); inspect each finished drawing rather than assuming projection guarantees fidelity. These rules govern placement; the feature forms come from the treatment and the character (Original: dot eyes and a small wedge nose; Detailed: shaped eyes with directed pupils and a readable identity-specific nose; approved characters: their own construction).

- **Front:** both eyes on one eye line, evenly spaced about the face's centre line; the nose on that centre line; the mouth centred under it. Cheek spots, if any, come in a matching pair.
- **Three-quarter:** the face turns toward one side. Both eyes stay on one eye line; the far eye sits nearer the far edge and may be smaller. The nose may break the far contour on that side only; the mouth shifts toward the far side; an ear, if shown, is on the near side.
- **Profile:** one eye; nose, lips and chin break the contour on the facing side; no second eye or front-view mouth.
- The eye line follows the head: a deliberate tilt or perspective turns the whole line with the head. Perspective and character-specific anatomy may place features; a tilt is never a reason to slide one feature out of the head's view.
- Keep features inside the head shape, except the nose tip (three-quarter, profile) and lips/chin (profile).
- An open ink contour traces the face's own edge (a jaw or cheek turn). No line crosses the face or cuts between features.
- Hair is a shell around the skull with an opening where the face shows, plus masses or groups pinned to it. From the side and back the shell covers the skull; never draw a bald skull with hair stuck behind it. In a front view hair does not cover an eye unless that is a deliberate, stated choice.
- Put asymmetry in the head shape, the hair and the gesture, not in scattered features. Tilt or turn the whole head (in rigs, rotate the head bone) rather than sliding features apart.
- In rigs, keep every facial mark in the head's group so nods and tilts move the face as one piece.
- Check each face at three-times zoom before delivery: say which way it looks. If eyes, nose and mouth disagree, redraw that face before anything else.

## Workflow

1. Resolve the treatment before selecting visual inputs. Detailed: read `references/detailed-character-style.md`, view the primary scene, and attach that same asset. Original: inspect the historical pair and use `references/style-guide.md` for alternatives. Inspect every photo/edit target if not already seen.
2. Label input roles explicitly. Do not infer authorship from uploader names. Reference-sheet captions are supplied labels, not independent provenance verification.
3. Extract the essential content in a short brief: who/what, action, relationship, identifying cues, and setting markers. Limit photo-detail inventory. Retain modern clothes and objects unless asked to change them. Preserve exact requested text and user invariants, but represent identifying detail with economical shapes.
4. Plan silhouette/gesture and negative space first, ink/fill balance second, palette third, texture last. Preserve pose and basic framing where useful; simplify background and internal contours without asking. Do not switch subjects or change the depicted action.
5. Construct a short prompt using the core brief and scaffold below. Carry the actual drawing mechanics into the generator prompt; do not reduce the skill to its name or a generic retro adjective. Assign each image a role. Prioritize reference geometry over source-photo anatomy and rendering. Do not lean on artist-name prompting.
6. For raster output, generate/edit with the image tool the host actually exposes, following the host adapter and the tool's current schema. Include target and selected style references, or ask for missing required images. Request real transparency only when asked.
7. Inspect the output against the selected treatment for drift across the entire visible cast, then compare geometry, single-view face construction, facial economy, ink/fill balance, shallow space, flat color, and selective or absent texture against that treatment's visual inputs. Detailed must pass the construction review in `references/detailed-character-style.md`, not just a flat-color or anatomy check. Use the guide's rubric when accessible. Compare saved recurring characters with their approved anchors, and invented recurring characters with their provisional briefs/artwork, together with the nearest relevant appearances. If a major category or identity invariant fails, make a focused corrective revision preserving the subject and all unaffected people/details, then recheck the changed and protected features. Reuse valid reviews for unchanged bytes. Prefer concrete corrections ("flatten shading, remove half the surface marks, keep rocket and layout unchanged") over "make it more retro." Do not claim inspection without seeing the output or promise guaranteed consistency.
8. Return the actual generated image through the host's display mechanism, or deliver the validated native files for a vector/rig/design-system workflow. Follow the host's saving policy; keep commentary brief. Do not deliver only a prompt or vector placeholder when an illustration was requested, unless the host adapter says no generator exists and you have said so.
